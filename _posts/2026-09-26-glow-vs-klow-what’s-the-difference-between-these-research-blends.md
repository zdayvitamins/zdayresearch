## 🧬 GLOW vs. KLOW: The Quick Answer

At first glance, GLOW and KLOW look like two completely different research blends. They're actually closely related.

**GLOW:** GHK-Cu + BPC-157 + TB-500  
**KLOW:** GHK-Cu + BPC-157 + TB-500 + KPV

So yes—the big difference is essentially **one extra ingredient: KPV.**

## 🔬 What Do the Three Shared Components Do?

The compounds shared by both blends have been investigated individually across several areas of research.

**GHK-Cu** is a naturally occurring copper-binding peptide studied in areas including tissue remodeling and skin biology.

**BPC-157** has attracted research interest for tissue-repair mechanisms, although much of the evidence comes from animal models rather than established human clinical trials.

**TB-500** is associated with thymosin beta-4 research involving cell migration, angiogenesis and tissue repair. An important distinction is that some research involves full-length thymosin beta-4 rather than TB-500 itself.

## ➕ So What Does KPV Add?

This is where KLOW gets its extra **K**.

KPV stands for the amino-acid sequence **Lys-Pro-Val**. It is a small fragment derived from alpha-MSH that has been investigated for its effects on inflammatory signaling.

That gives researchers another pathway to examine alongside the three compounds already found in GLOW.

## 🤔 Does That Mean KLOW Is "Better"?

Not scientifically.

Adding another research compound makes KLOW **different**, not automatically better. Research on the individual components cannot tell us exactly how the complete blends compare.

There are currently no controlled head-to-head studies demonstrating that GLOW or KLOW produces a superior outcome as a combination. That distinction matters when separating published research from assumptions based on the ingredients.

## 🧪 The Easy Way to Remember It

Think of it this way:

**GLOW = 3 components**  
GHK-Cu + BPC-157 + TB-500

**KLOW = GLOW + KPV**

One extra letter. One extra research component. And a surprisingly easy way to mix the names up. 😂
