🧬 **WHAT IS IGF-1?**

IGF-1 (insulin-like growth factor 1) is a naturally occurring signaling molecule that plays an important role in growth, cell activity and metabolism. It works through the IGF-1 receptor, triggering signaling pathways inside cells that researchers study across areas including muscle, bone and metabolic biology.

GH signaling stimulates the production of IGF-1, primarily in the liver, although other tissues can produce IGF-1 locally. IGF-1 then binds to the IGF-1 receptor (IGF-1R), activating signaling pathways involved in cell growth, survival and metabolism.

IGF-1 signaling is studied for its role in growth, bone and muscle biology, tissue development and metabolism. Because the IGF-1 pathway affects many different cell types, researchers continue to investigate how this signaling system works throughout the body.

Growth hormone (GH) and IGF-1 are closely connected, but they behave differently. GH is released in pulses, while circulating IGF-1 levels are generally more stable. This is one reason IGF-1 is useful to researchers studying growth-hormone signaling.

🔬 **WHAT DOES IGF-1 STAND FOR?**

IGF-1 stands for insulin-like growth factor 1. The name comes from some structural and signaling similarities to insulin, although IGF-1 and insulin have distinct biological roles.
