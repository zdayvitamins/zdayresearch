🧬 **ANOTHER SIDE OF MOTS-C RESEARCH**

MOTS-c is usually discussed in the context of mitochondria and metabolism, but a 2026 study explored something very different.

Researchers described MOTS-c as a mitochondrial-encoded host defense peptide and studied antibacterial and immunomodulatory activity. The work included *E. coli*, MRSA, human monocytes/macrophages, and a mouse model of acute infection.

That's a pretty interesting reminder that mitochondrial biology reaches well beyond energy production.

The important context: much of this evidence came from cell and animal experiments. It does **not** establish MOTS-c as a treatment for infections in people.

Stay curious, research friends. 🧬
